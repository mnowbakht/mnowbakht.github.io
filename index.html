About

I am a psycholinguist with a PhD in Applied Linguistics, working as a postdoctoral researcher and lecturer in the Neuro-Psycholinguistics Lab at Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf. My research asks how the way we become bilingual shapes the mind: I compare simultaneous bilinguals, sequential bilinguals and monolinguals to understand how language experience relates to executive function.

Before coming to Düsseldorf, I was Assistant Professor of English at the Faculty of Medicine, Shahid Beheshti University of Medical Sciences in Tehran, and a visiting researcher at CeRCA (CNRS, University of Poitiers) in France. That background in medicine is now shaping a new direction in my work: clinical psycholinguistics, where language measures can serve as practical, non-invasive tools in healthcare.

Alongside research, I teach psycholinguistics, bilingualism and English for Medicine at HHU, and Business English at CBS International Business School.

Research

Bilingualism and executive function

How do the age and context in which a second language is acquired relate to cognitive control? My current project compares simultaneous bilinguals, sequential bilinguals and monolinguals, using reaction-time tasks, tests and questionnaires that I design and program in Python for online delivery.

Working memory and second language processing

My earlier work examined the cognitive side of language learning: how working memory, proficiency and age shape the processing of anaphoric sentences, how working memory and error type determine whether written corrective feedback works, and how individual differences such as intelligence and personality relate to speaking ability.

Clinical psycholinguistics

I am developing research on language and cognition in multiple sclerosis, including whether bilingual experience contributes to cognitive reserve, and whether tip-of-the-tongue states can act as an early, low-cost marker of lexical decline. The aim is research with direct clinical value, such as screening tools and non-pharmacological approaches.

Projects

Bilingualism and executive function

Postdoctoral research project · Neuro-Psycholinguistics Lab, Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf · 2024 – present

Does it matter for the mind whether two languages are learned from birth or later in life? This project compares simultaneous bilinguals, sequential bilinguals and monolinguals on measures of executive function such as inhibition, switching and working memory.

  • Design and programming of online experiments (reaction-time tasks, tests and questionnaires) in Python
  • Participant recruitment and management of project resources
  • Data analysis with Python, R and SPSS
